# Responsible AI Case Study Report

## Case Study

Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149

## Responsible AI Issue

AI / chatbot misinformation and organizational accountability.

## Background

In November 2022, Jake Moffatt used a chatbot available on Air Canada's website while researching bereavement fares.

The chatbot indicated that a customer who had already travelled could apply for a reduced bereavement fare within 90 days of the ticket being issued.

However, Air Canada's official bereavement policy stated that the reduced fare could not be requested retroactively after travel had been completed.

The customer relied on the chatbot's information, purchased tickets at the normal price, and later requested the reduced fare.

Air Canada refused the request.

## What Happened

The information provided by the chatbot conflicted with Air Canada's official policy.

The dispute eventually reached the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal.

The Tribunal concluded that Air Canada was responsible for the information provided through its website chatbot.

The company could not avoid responsibility simply because the inaccurate information had been produced by an automated system.

The Tribunal found Air Canada liable for negligent misrepresentation.

## Important Technical Note

The public record does not establish that the chatbot was necessarily a modern generative Large Language Model.

Therefore, this case should primarily be described as an AI/chatbot misinformation and accountability incident rather than definitively calling it an LLM hallucination.

## Impact

The incident had several consequences.

### Customer Impact

The customer relied on incorrect information when making a financial decision.

### Business Impact

Air Canada became responsible for damages resulting from information supplied by its automated customer-service system.

### Trust Impact

Incorrect automated advice can reduce customer trust in AI-powered customer support.

### Legal and Governance Impact

The case demonstrates that organizations remain responsible for AI systems that interact with customers.

Deploying an automated system does not remove organizational accountability.

## Responsible AI Lessons

Companies must treat AI-generated customer information as part of their official service.

AI systems should not provide policy information unless that information is grounded in an approved and current source.

High-impact requests should include confidence checks or human review.

AI responses should be monitored and tested regularly.

Organizations should maintain consistency between chatbot responses and official documentation.

## Relevance to HearMe

HearMe is designed around AI-powered customer communication.

A similar failure could occur if the system provides incorrect information about:

- Company policies
- Refunds
- Customer accounts
- Payments
- Product information
- Service conditions

Therefore, HearMe should retrieve information from approved company documentation and escalate sensitive cases when confidence is insufficient.

## Conclusion

The Air Canada case demonstrates that AI automation does not transfer responsibility away from the company operating the system.

Organizations must ensure that AI outputs are accurate, monitored, grounded in reliable information, and subject to appropriate human oversight.
